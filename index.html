Who I am

Most of what I've built exists because I needed it in a real business first.

I started in recruitment. That is where you learn, quickly and unforgivingly, that most commercial problems are people problems wearing a different coat. A pipeline that stalls, a team that churns, a manager who was promoted and never taught how to manage: these are the same problem viewed from different corners of the business.

So I stopped advising and started operating. I have run multi-million pound businesses, managed hundreds of staff across multiple sectors, and worked with everyone from entry-level hires to the C-suite. Running something is a different discipline from recommending it. You carry the payroll, the compliance, the client who is unhappy on a Friday afternoon, and the decision nobody else wants to make.

Easy Talent came out of that. Then the products came out of Easy Talent, because the same friction kept appearing and buying software to fix it kept disappointing me. If you have run the business, you know which problems are actually worth solving, and you build a narrower, more useful thing than somebody designing from the outside.

That is still how I work. I operate businesses, I build the systems they need, and a small number of times a year I go and do the same thing inside somebody else's company.

03
Recruitment intelligence · product

Recruitment Intelligence

Would your CV actually get shortlisted for this job?

The problem. Candidates are told to "tailor your CV" with no idea what that means against a specific job. Employers write a job description in ten minutes and then wonder why the applications are wrong. Both sides are guessing at the same document, and neither is ever shown the judgement that decides the outcome.

Why I built it. Running a recruitment business means reading CVs against real roles every day, and writing the roles those CVs are answering. The read that produces a shortlist is not complicated and it is not a secret. It is just never written down for the person it affects. A candidate finds out by silence; an employer finds out by a month of wrong applications. Both of those are avoidable before anything is sent.

What it is. Put in a CV and the exact role, and get an evidence-based read on where it genuinely matches, where it does not, the shortlist risks, and the changes worth making, with a tailored CV rebuilt only from evidence already in your own. It will not invent experience you do not have, because that is what gets found out at interview. Employers get the same judgement in reverse: a role analysis, a job description people understand, screening criteria and interview questions. Recruitment judgement applied to a specific job, not a generic AI rewrite.

How I work

I got tired of AI tools that knew nothing about the rest of the business.

Most businesses already hold what they need to know. It sits in inboxes, spreadsheets, last quarter's decisions and somebody's head. The tools bolted on top know none of it, so every question starts from zero, the same decisions get made twice, and nothing compounds.

So I started building an operating layer instead of buying more tools. Information becomes intelligence, intelligence becomes capability, capability produces action, and the result of that action is kept rather than thrown away.

Information→ Intelligence→ Capability→ Action→ Learning
